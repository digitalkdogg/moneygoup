DeepMoney API

Smart Stock Discovery Engine

Requirements & Upgrade Specification

Version 2.0 | February 2026 | Includes AI / Tech Stock Discovery Module

Revision Note

This document supersedes both the initial v2.0 draft and the first
AI/Tech revision. All original requirements are retained. The primary
change in this revision is the replacement of the static AI/Tech Curated
Watchlist (Section 6.2) with a Dynamic Discovery Screener. This redesign
addresses the core concern that a static watchlist of well-known names
(NVDA, PLTR, etc.) would simply resurface large-cap stocks already
visible to any investor — defeating the purpose of discovery. The new
approach is signal-driven, market-cap-bounded, and biased toward
emerging and under-the-radar opportunities.

1.  Overview

The current DeepMoney API performs basic NLP-based entity extraction and
keyword sentiment analysis on news articles to surface trending stocks
and market sectors. Version 2.0 upgrades it into a Smart Stock Discovery
Engine that identifies stocks with strong 1-year growth potential,
priced under $150/share, with a verifiable track record of growth or
demonstrated stability.

This revision extends the scope to include a dedicated AI and Technology
Stock Discovery Module with a core design principle: the engine must
surface stocks that investors are unlikely to already know about.
Large-cap household names (NVDA, MSFT, PLTR, AAPL) may appear via the
general news pipeline if they are genuinely trending, but the AI/Tech
module is explicitly designed to find emerging, under-the-radar, and
newly-relevant opportunities in the AI and technology space.

2.  Goals & Success Criteria

2.1 Primary Goals

-   Surface stocks under $150/share with strong 1-year forward growth
    potential.

-   Distinguish true growth stocks from noise by combining news signals
    with fundamental financial data.

-   Identify both established growth stocks and up-and-coming stable
    stocks across all sectors.

-   Discovery principle: Proactively surface emerging AI/Tech stocks
    that are not yet on most investors’ radar — explicitly avoiding
    over-indexing on large-cap names.

-   Reduce false positives through multi-dimensional GPS scoring.

3.  Current State & Limitations

The current implementation has the following known limitations that this
upgrade will address:

-   No financial data integration — the API only uses news articles with
    no access to price, revenue, earnings, or valuation data.

-   Weak ticker resolution — organization name matching is fragile; many
    mentioned companies are missed or incorrectly mapped.

-   No price filter — stocks of any price are returned.

-   No growth qualification — no mechanism to determine whether a stock
    has a history of growth.

-   No AI/Tech vertical awareness — the API cannot proactively find
    relevant stocks in fast-moving technology verticals.

-   Bias toward large, well-covered companies — stocks with the most
    news coverage score highest, which naturally advantages mega-cap
    names regardless of growth potential.

4.  Stock Universe & Growth Classification

All stocks — regardless of how they are discovered — must pass the same
price filter and be classified into one of the following growth profiles
before receiving a GPS score.

4.1 Price Filter (Hard Gate — All Stocks)

-   Any stock at or above $150/share is excluded before scoring.
    Configurable via PRICE_FILTER_MAX env var.

-   Price check occurs immediately after ticker resolution, before any
    enrichment API calls.

4.2 Growth Classifications

Profile A — Established Growth Stock

-   Revenue growth ≥ 10% YoY in at least 2 of the last 3 fiscal years.

-   EPS growth positive in at least 2 of the last 3 fiscal years.

-   52-week price return ≥ 10%.

-   Current price < $150/share.

Profile B — Up-and-Coming Stable Stock

-   Revenue growth ≥ 15% in the most recent fiscal year.

-   Market cap between $300M and $10B (small-to-mid cap).

-   Analyst consensus 1-year price target implies ≥ 15% upside.

-   No more than 2 consecutive quarters of negative gross margin.

-   Current price < $150/share.

Profile C — AI/Tech Hyper-Growth Stock

NEW PROFILE

A third classification for AI and technology companies that relaxes
profitability requirements in recognition that high-growth tech
companies commonly reinvest aggressively in R&D.

-   Sector must be AI, Semiconductor, Cloud/SaaS, Cybersecurity, or Data
    Infrastructure.

-   Revenue growth ≥ 20% in the most recent fiscal year.

-   Gross margin ≥ 50% (scalable, IP-driven business model).

-   Analyst consensus 1-year price target implies ≥ 20% upside.

-   R&D spend as % of revenue ≥ 10%.

-   Current price < $150/share.

6.  AI / Technology Stock Discovery Module

NEW MODULE

This module runs in parallel with the news-driven ticker pipeline and
uses three complementary discovery strategies: an AI/Tech keyword signal
layer, a Dynamic Discovery Screener that queries financial APIs for
qualifying stocks from a broad universe, and a curated Emerging Player
Watchlist scoped exclusively to small and mid-cap companies not yet on
most investors’ radar. Large-cap household names (NVDA, MSFT, PLTR) are
not excluded — they may still appear via the news pipeline — but receive
a GPS penalty that systematically deprioritizes them in favor of less
well-known opportunities.

6.1 Discovery Strategy A — AI/Tech Keyword Signal Layer

The existing news article pipeline is extended with an AI/Tech-specific
keyword taxonomy. When an article triggers a keyword match, all resolved
tickers from that article receive an AI/Tech Signal Bonus in GPS scoring
(Section 7.2). Sub-sector sentiment is tracked independently.

6.2 Discovery Strategy B — Dynamic AI/Tech Screener

REDESIGNED

Rather than a static watchlist of well-known tickers, the engine uses a
Dynamic Discovery Screener that queries financial data APIs each cycle
to identify qualifying AI/Tech stocks that pass all discovery filters.
This approach is signal-driven and self-updating — it finds what is
relevant now rather than what was relevant when the list was last
edited.

Design rationale: A static watchlist seeded with large-cap names (NVDA,
PLTR, DDOG, CRWD) would dominate results every cycle regardless of
current conditions, because those companies have the strongest
fundamentals and most news coverage by definition. The screener instead
discovers from the bottom up — filtering a broad universe down to
genuinely interesting, under-the-radar candidates.

Screener Universe

The screener operates against a broad universe of publicly traded US
equities tagged with an AI or technology sector classification by the
data provider (FMP Company Profile sector field). This universe is
re-fetched weekly and cached; individual stock data within it is fetched
on-demand per cycle.

Screener Filter Chain

Stocks in the universe are passed through the following filter chain in
order. A stock must pass all filters to advance.

The discovery signals are intentionally biased toward recency and
momentum rather than size. A $500M company that just received its first
analyst upgrade and reported 40% revenue growth will rank above a $40B
company with the same fundamentals but no new signals.

Screener Output

-   The screener returns up to 10 candidate tickers per cycle after
    applying all filters and ranking by discovery signals.

-   These candidates are passed into the shared fundamental enrichment
    pipeline (Stage 4) and must still qualify under Profile A, B, or C
    to appear in results.

-   The screener runs once per API cycle and results are cached for 30
    minutes to avoid hammering financial data APIs on concurrent
    requests.

-   If the screener returns fewer than 3 candidates in a cycle (e.g.,
    market holiday, data outage), the engine logs a warning and returns
    whatever is available without erroring.

Screener Scope

-   The screener does not give preferential treatment to any ticker by
    name. NVDA and a $300M AI startup are evaluated on identical
    criteria.

-   No ticker is guaranteed to appear in screener results — inclusion is
    earned each cycle based on current signals.

6.3 Discovery Strategy C — Emerging Player Watchlist

REINSTATED — SCOPED

A curated watchlist is maintained as a JSON config file
(ai_tech_watchlist.json) and scanned each cycle. Unlike a broad static
list, this watchlist is strictly scoped: it contains only small and
mid-cap companies (market cap < $10B) that the team believes are
emerging players worth monitoring — companies that are unlikely to
surface consistently via news or the screener alone due to limited
analyst coverage or low news volume.

Scope constraint: The watchlist must NEVER contain mega-cap or
well-known large-cap stocks. If a company’s market cap exceeds $10B at
time of scan, it is automatically skipped and flagged for removal from
the list. The watchlist is for discovery — not for tracking stocks
already on every investor’s radar.

Watchlist Entry Requirements

-   Each entry must include: ticker symbol, company name, AI/Tech
    sub-sector tag, and a brief rationale note explaining why it was
    added.

-   At time of addition, the company must have market cap < $10B and
    current price < $150/share.

-   Analyst coverage of 5 or fewer analysts preferred — the watchlist
    targets stocks most investors haven’t found yet.

-   Entries where market cap has grown above $10B are auto-flagged for
    human review and removal in the next monthly update.

Watchlist Pipeline Behavior

-   Watchlist tickers are passed through the same price filter →
    fundamental enrichment → growth classification → GPS scoring
    pipeline as all other tickers.

-   Watchlist tickers receive the same Emerging Player GPS bonus as
    screener-discovered tickers (see Section 7.2).

-   A watchlist ticker that fails growth classification (Profile A, B,
    or C) is excluded from results for that cycle — being on the
    watchlist does not guarantee inclusion.

-   The watchlist is reviewed and updated monthly by the engineering or
    product team.

6.4 AI/Tech Sub-Sector Sentiment Tracking

The API tracks sentiment independently for each AI/Tech sub-sector
defined in Section 6.1, producing a hot_ai_sectors response field
alongside hot_markets.

-   Sub-sector sentiment uses the same sentiment.analyze() method as
    existing industry sentiment.

-   Only articles triggering at least one keyword from a sub-sector
    contribute to that sub-sector’s score.

-   Top 2 AI/Tech sub-sectors by average sentiment are returned in
    hot_ai_sectors.

-   Sub-sector sentiment feeds as an input signal into the AI/Tech GPS
    bonus (Section 7.2).

7.  Multi-Factor GPS Scoring Engine

All qualifying candidate stocks receive a composite Growth Potential
Score (GPS) between 0 and 100, used to rank and return the top results.
The engine includes an AI/Tech Signal Bonus layer for emerging
technology stocks and a Large-Cap Penalty that deprioritizes well-known
mega-cap names without fully excluding them.

9.  Improved Ticker Resolution

-   Integrate a dedicated company-to-ticker resolution service (OpenFIGI
    or FMP /search).

-   Cache resolved ticker mappings for 24 hours.

-   Handle AI/Tech-specific aliases — e.g., ‘Google DeepMind’ → GOOGL
    (but note GOOGL will be filtered out by the market cap ceiling in
    the screener).

-   Gracefully skip unresolvable or private company names (e.g., OpenAI,
    Anthropic, xAI) — log a skip event, do not error.

-   Support disambiguation — prefer higher market-cap entity when a name
    matches multiple tickers.

-   Fall back to NLP-based matching only when the resolution service
    returns no result.

10. Caching & Performance

-   Fundamental data per ticker cached for minimum 1 hour.

-   News article fetching and NLP processing must complete within 1.5
    seconds.

-   Financial data enrichment must complete within 1.5 seconds for up to
    20 candidate tickers in parallel.

-   Updated: The Dynamic Screener universe (broad sector ticker list) is
    cached for 7 days and refreshed weekly.

-   New: Per-cycle screener results (filtered candidates) are cached for
    30 minutes to protect downstream API rate limits.

-   Redis cache or equivalent for all price, fundamentals, and
    resolution caching.

11. Non-Functional Requirements

11.1 Security

-   All external API keys stored as environment variables — never
    hardcoded.

-   Existing NextAuth session check and checkOrigin() middleware
    preserved.

-   Rate limiting applied to prevent abuse of downstream financial data
    APIs.

11.2 Observability

-   Log GPS score breakdown including AI/Tech bonus breakdown per
    returned ticker.

-   Log discovery_source for every result (news, screener, or both).

-   Track and alert if large-cap stocks (market cap > $50B) appear in
    hot_ai_tech_stocks — this indicates a logic regression.

-   Emit structured logs with ticker, classification, sub-sector,
    latencies, and cache hit/miss ratios.

-   /health endpoint validates connectivity to all downstream APIs.

11.3 Maintainability

-   DataProvider interface abstracts all financial data fetching —
    sources swappable without logic changes.

-   Scoring weights and AI/Tech bonus values externalized to config /
    env vars.

-   AI/Tech keyword taxonomy stored in JSON config — editable without
    code change.

-   Screener filter thresholds (market cap ceiling, revenue growth
    floor, gross margin floor) stored in config — tunable without
    redeployment.

-   All new modules must have unit tests with ≥ 80% code coverage.

14. Out of Scope (v2.0)

-   Short selling or bearish stock signals.

-   Options or derivatives data.

-   User-customizable watchlists or portfolio tracking (planned for
    v3.0).

-   Real-time streaming / WebSocket price updates.

-   International (non-US) equities.

-   Private company or pre-IPO AI/Tech companies — gracefully skipped if
    encountered by the NLP pipeline.

-   A manually maintained static ticker list — intentionally excluded
    from this design.
